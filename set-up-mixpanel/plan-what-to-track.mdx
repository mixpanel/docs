Your plan decides what data you'll have to work with. Your engineer builds from it, and your reports can only answer questions your events cover. Start with a short list tied to your top questions, not everything you could track.

**Done when:**

- You've picked your first events
- Each one answers a top question
- They're written down

<Tip>
  Using an AI coding agent? Follow [Install with AI](/docs/quickstart/install-with-ai) instead. It plans and installs in one session.
</Tip>

## Set up yourself

### Decide what to measure

Start from the questions you want answered, then pick the fewest events that answer them. [What to Track](/docs/what-to-track) · [Measure Value](/guides/strategic-playbooks/guide-to-product-analytics/measure-value)

Selling to companies? Decide whether to analyze them as groups, not just as users. [Group Analytics](/docs/data-structure/group-analytics)

### Learn the building blocks

New to events, users, and properties? [Data Model](/docs/data-structure/concepts) · [Events](/docs/data-structure/events-and-properties)

### Choose what to leave out

Decide which events earn their place. Skip personal data you don't need, and properties Mixpanel already collects. [How To Build a Tracking Strategy](/guides/guides-by-workflow/build-tracking-strategy) · [Data Privacy & PII Best Practices](/guides/guides-by-workflow/data-privacy) · [Default Properties](/docs/data-structure/property-reference/default-properties)

### Write your tracking plan

Start from a template for SaaS, retail, media, or financial services, or begin blank. [Create A Tracking Plan](/docs/tracking-best-practices/tracking-plan)

### Get a second opinion

Share your draft with your Mixpanel onboarding contact, if you have one, or with a teammate who knows your product.

## Next step

When your plan is written down, go to [2. Implement and test in development](/guides/get-started/implement-and-test).
